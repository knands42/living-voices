# Living Voices — UI Design Style Guide

A design spec for **Living Voices**, a local roleplay chat app: the UI of a
candlelit library at night where players step into a story as characters in
a party. Reference mockups: chat screen, home/Adventures screen, and the
Characters showcase gallery.

## Terminology

Use **adventure** everywhere (UI copy, identifiers, Rust types) — never
"campaign." A group of characters is the party/cast; a player persona is a
character. Story time-of-day/progress state is the Story Clock.

## Typography

A warm, reading-first hierarchy stitched from two families: a serif (EB
Garamond or Cinzel) for headers, brand, and nav labels — with a small caps
feel on nav items — gives the app its stories-and-aldermen gravitas, while a
humanist sans (Noto Sans or Source Sans) carries the chat body, name labels
inside bubbles, and timestamps so the conversation stays effortless to read.
Narrator lines flex the sans family into italic with extra line height, so
scene direction reads as voice-over rather than dialogue.

## Theme & Mood

At its heart this is a dark warm fantasy UI built around one atmosphere: a
candlelit library at night, where parchment and aged wood textures live as
soft backdrops — long-shadowed, low-opacity, with floating dust motes and a
subtle candle-glow vignette — while every interactive element (bubbles,
buttons, inputs, cards) stays clean, flat, and modern for unimpeded
readability. The aesthetic deliberately avoids heavy ornamentation on
controls, so the old-world feel is carried by the atmosphere and the
accents, not by the chrome.

## Color Palette

An earth-and-warm-gold palette in which the depths stay dark and the light
is paper and candle: the chat backdrop is a smoked parchment cream, text is
either dark ink on parchment or cream on dark, and the accent language is
almost entirely gold — active states, brand, hover rings — with amber-glow
for candle-like sparks and bronze for borders, secondary buttons, and genre
sigils. The flat, modern interactive layer (pill chips, toggles, badges)
uses the same warm earthtones squared off into clean, legible shapes, never
ornamented.
